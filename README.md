# Braided-Symmetric Derivations of Algebras

Author: Abderrahim Adrabi, Mohammed V University in Rabat.  
Current version: **v69**.

## The idea of the paper

We study braided-symmetric derivations of an algebra over a field. These are maps that satisfy the Leibniz rule for the product symmetrized by an involution of A ⊗ A. The choice of involution decides which derivations appear. The identity involution gives the ordinary derivations when 2 is invertible. The swap gives the Jordan derivations. The negative of the identity gives all linear endomorphisms, and the negative of the swap gives the Lie derivations. We then twist the swap by an involutive automorphism or anti-automorphism. The Leibniz defect of a map decides when it is a twisted derivation. For a commutative reduced algebra with 2 invertible, the twisted maps that commute with the automorphism are exactly the derivations that commute with it. On the algebra F_3[ε]/(ε³) the twisted derivations and the α-derivations do not contain each other. An example with nilpotent elements shows that the reduced hypothesis is needed, an example in characteristic 2 shows that the invertibility of 2 is needed, and a four-dimensional example shows that reducedness and commutativity cannot be dropped together.

## The formalized background

`lean/Background/` formalizes the paper's sixteen
bibliography entries, one module per cluster and a shared prelude:

* the definitions the paper takes from each entry, in the paper's conventions;
* the lemmas of each entry that are elementary enough to prove — the
  linearisation of Herstein's Jordan condition, `δ ∘ δ = 0` in degrees 0–3 with
  the Hochschild coboundary `δ_n` defined in every degree, "the 1-cocycles are
  the derivations", "the transpose is an involution", the Jordan identity for
  quasi-multiplication;
* a witness wherever the reference asserts that a distinction is non-vacuous: a
  Jordan derivation that is not a derivation (characteristic 2), a
  braided-symmetric derivation that is not a braided derivation (the signed
  swap), a ring that is not reduced;
* the three deep theorems the paper uses as hypotheses — Herstein 1957,
  Theorem 3.1; Cusack 1975, Corollary 5; Brešar 1988, Theorem 1 — as exactly
  stated `Prop`-valued definitions that every consumer carries as a hypothesis.

Nothing there uses `sorry`, `admit`, a custom `axiom` or `native_decide`. Build
it with `lean/build_references.sh`; the inventory of what each module proves and
what it assumes, together with the places where the paper's attribution and the
reference's own statement differ, is in
`lean/BACKGROUND.md`.

- **v67** (2026-11-02): one paragraph added to Remark 4.6, no theorem changes. It records what the proof of Theorem 4.1 leaves open when commutativity is dropped and reducedness kept. With $E$ the Leibniz defect of $D$ and $E_{\mathrm a}(x,y) = E(x,y) - E(y,x)$, one has $E_{\mathrm a}(x,y) = D([x,y]) - [D(x),y] - [x,D(y)]$, so $E_{\mathrm a} = 0$ says that $D$ is a derivation for the commutator bracket, and $E_{\mathrm a}$ takes values in $A_+$. Because $\alpha$ is multiplicative and $D$ commutes with it, $E$ maps $A_i \times A_j$ into $A_{i+j}$, so with $A = A_+ + A_-$ three cases remain: at a mixed pair $E(x,y)$ and $E(y,x)$ both lie in $A_-$ and $E_{\mathrm a}$ vanishes; on $A_+ \times A_+$ the restriction of $D$ is a Jordan derivation of the reduced, semiprime, $2$-torsion-free ring $A_+$, hence a derivation there by Theorem 3.4, so $E$ vanishes. What is left is $A_- \times A_-$, where $E$ is alternating with values in $A_+$; if it vanishes there the defect is symmetric and the end of the proof of Theorem 4.1 applies. Whether it must vanish is not settled in the paper. Lean: the paragraph is kernel-checked in `Reduction.lean` except for the sentence it leaves open — `Ea_eq_lie`, `Ea_alpha_fixed`, `Ed_alpha_of_*`, `Ea_eq_zero_of_fixed_skew` for the mixed pairs, and `fixedSubring`/`Dfix`/`jder_Dfix`/`Ed_eq_zero_of_fixed` for the claim that the restriction of $D$ to $A_+$ is a Jordan derivation there, hence a derivation by Bresar's theorem (`Bresar1988.lean`); `thm41_of_odd_odd` assembles them, so the only remaining input is the odd-odd case.
