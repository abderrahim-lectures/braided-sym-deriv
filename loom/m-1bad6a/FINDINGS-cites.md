Conventions used below.

- **Pages are the printed pages**, recovered from the running heads and feet that survive in the OCR (`1004  M. breSar`, `JORDAN DERIVATIONS ON PRIME RINGS  1105`, `1.1  Complexes of R-Modules  3`, …). Where a text file carries no page markers I say so and give the section or numbered item instead.
- **Quotes are verbatim from the text file**, OCR errors included. Only two things are normalised: runs of spaces are collapsed, and a word hyphenated at the end of an OCR line is joined (`opera-/tions`, `mod-/ule`, `F-subalge-/bra`). Every character-level repair needed to *read* the scan is named in `[OCR: …]`; nothing else is changed and nothing is quoted from memory.
- ` / ` inside a quotation marks a line break of a displayed formula; `…` marks an elision.
- A hit for the *bibliography* entry, or a similar statement in another section, is not taken as support. `OK` is used only where the named passage itself carries the fact.
- `LOCATOR-WRONG` means the fact is in the source but at another place, which is then given.

## Recommended changes

1. **Line 321** — `and $\delta \circ \delta = 0$ \citep[\S9.1]{Weibel1994}`: §9.1 constructs the Hochschild complex but never states `δ∘δ = 0`; that is the definition of a cochain complex in Weibel §1.1, p. 3, which also defines `n`-cocycle, `n`-coboundary and `H^n`. Change the locator to `\citep[\S\S1.1 and 9.1]{Weibel1994}`. The same edit covers line 323 (`n`-cocycles modulo images `δg`) and line 332 ("$E_D$ is a cocycle of the Hochschild complex"), because the paper defines `n`-cocycle itself at line 322 and cohomology at line 323; no second locator change is needed there.
2. **Line 278** — `in the sense of \citet[\S1.2]{Herstein1976}`: the locator is plausibly right (the definition of an involution is at §1.2, p. 17) but its three displayed conditions did not survive the OCR of the copy on disk, so the sentence cannot be checked against it. Either keep it and accept one unverified attribution, or add a checkable source: Bresar–Zelmanov 2025, Example 2.2, p. 205 — "an involution on an associative algebra A is a linear antiautomorphism ∗ : A → A that satisfies (x∗ )∗ = x for every x ∈ A" — which carries `a** = a`, additivity and `(ab)* = b*a*` at once.
3. **Lines 494, 604, 607** — the four `Pierce1982` calls (`\S3.5`, `\S4.4`, `\S3.5`, `\S12.6`): no change can be justified from what is on disk. The section titles in the table of contents fit the uses, but no line of §3.5, §4.4 or §12.6 was read, so all four stay unverified. If the author has the book, replace the section locators by the numbered items actually used — those are what a reviewer will check; otherwise source the four facts to references in `loom-pack`.
4. **No change, recorded as checked:** line 90's two-part claim (`\S\S2 and 4`) — §2 defines cocycles and §4 identifies 1-cocycles with derivations — and line 93's priority claim (Beidar et al. 2001, Theorem 4.3, p. 259, is earlier than Benkovič 2015) both hold as written.
5. **No change, for the record:** `Huru2006` and `Benkovic2015` are cited by section and item because their text files carry no page markers (§4.1, Definition 14 is p. 26 of Lobachevskii J. Math. 24; the Benkovič remarks are in §1 of the Aequationes Math. paper). If printed pages are wanted throughout, those two need page numbers from the published versions, not from these files.
